About Me

Hello, I'm James Henry, a passionate Computer Engineering student at California State University, Long Beach, with a keen interest in embedded systems and software engineering. My academic journey has been both challenging and fulfilling, balancing rigorous coursework with hands-on projects that span from FPGA prototyping to PLC programming. Each experience has allowed me to deepen my technical expertise and contribute meaningfully to innovative solutions. Fueled by a love for technology and a commitment to continuous learning, I've immersed myself in various projects, including creating a Planet Simulator with Python, designing an analog line-following robot, and developing a Smart Car with embedded systems. My dedication extends beyond the classroom as I actively seek opportunities to apply my skills in real-world scenarios, from contributing to open-source projects to optimizing real-time monitoring systems during my internship. Let's connect and explore how we can collaborate on cutting-edge technologies and impactful projects!

Analog Line-Following Robot

Designed a 100% analog line-follower. It uses dual photoresistors, MOSFET transistors, and an H-bridge to control the direction and speed of the DC motors.

Smart Car

Designed a smart car using IR Sensors, Stepper Motors, a power supply circuit made of capacitors to smooth the noisy voltage signals, and TM4C Microcontroller programming in C. The car functions similarly to a micromouse and avoids hitting walls.

Etch-a-Sketch

Recreated Etch-a-Sketch (the classical drawing game) by creating a custom controller made of rotary encoders and resistors, using the Nexys A7-100T FPGA Board, Verilog Programming, and a VGA cable to output it to the monitor.

Banking Simulator

Designed a banking simulator that allows users to create various types of accounts. It uses Luhn's Algorithm to distinguish between real and fake credit cards, demonstrating expertise in Data Structures and Algorithms using C programming.

Infinity Mirror

Created and designed an infinity mirror using white pine wood, stain, LED strips, an acrylic mirror to replicate the infinite effect, a 6.5V battery pack, and current-limiting resistors.

Clock

Designed a clock using white pine wood, stain, aluminum arms, internal circuitry, and a 6V battery pack. This project taught me a lot about cost efficiency, design, and how analog circuits work in the world of engineering.
